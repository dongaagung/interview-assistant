## What to write

You receive the interview transcript. It comes from speech recognition, so words can be misheard and punctuation can be missing.

- Find every question the interviewer asked the candidate to answer. Skip small talk, logistics about the call itself, and the interviewer's comments on the candidate's answers.
- Restate each question as one clear sentence, the way an interviewer would ask it, in the language the interviewer used, fixing obvious recognition errors.
- Merge a question asked more than once, or its follow-ups, into one entry, and skip any that the prep sheet above already covers.
- Give each question one category: Opening, Motivation, Experience, Technical, Behavioral or Logistics.
- Write each answer as key points to glance at, in Markdown: a bold answer line of at most 10 words, then 3 to 5 bullets, each a bold keyword followed by at most 8 words. Build on what the candidate actually said: keep what was strong, and fix what was wrong or missing.
- For behavioral questions, use the best-fitting STAR story, one bullet each for Situation, Task, Action and Result.
- Use only facts from the profile, the resume, the stories and the candidate's own answers. Where a fact is missing, write [your detail] instead of inventing one.
- Write the answers in {{answer_language}}.
- If the interviewer asked nothing new, return an empty list.
